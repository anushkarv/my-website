About Me

Greetings! I am a 4th year undergraduate student pursuing Geophysics with the Earth and Planetary Science Department at UC Berkeley.

Currently, I am with Professor Burkhard Militzer’s group, using ab-initio DFT-MD and equation-of-state modeling to investigate the melting behavior and potential phase transitions of materials under high-pressure conditions.

I am a student researcher at the U.S. Geological Survey, Earthquake Science Center, advised by Dr. Annemarie Baltay, working on ground-motion modeling and source physics analysis in the Mendocino Fracture Zone.

Alongside my research, I am passionate about improving science education and policy - I teach and mentor students curious about physics and math, and assist with earthquake science outreach workshops. Fun fact, I actually used to be a Philosophy major and was interested in pursuing criminal law. I am ultimately grateful for my experiences philosophizing, as it taught me how to think analytically and ask questions (a little too much) about the world around me. Outside of my academic interests, I enjoy photography, hiking, writing short stories, and exploring different types of tea.
