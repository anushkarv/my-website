Research

High-pressure materials

High energy density science (HEDS) is an interdisciplinary field that spans throughout sub-fields of physics, material science, and planetary science. Understanding how materials behave at high-pressure and high-temperature is crucial for developing planetary interior models, superconductor research, and fusion energy.

Gold

Melting Curve of Gold

Melting curve calculations of gold at high-pressure is crucial as the material is often used as a pressure standard in extreme condition experiments, establishing accurate melting curves that can help resolve thermal structures of terrestrial planets and aid in capsule design for fusion research. Numerous shock compression and laser-driven diamond anvil cell experiments have explored phase behavior of fcc and bcc gold up to 2 TPa. Due to gold's fcc and bcc stability across large pressure regimes, experimental melting studies would require an immense amount of heat in order to accurately calculate the melting curve of Gold above 1 TPa. Computational methods such as DFT-MD are able to allow for a more feasible calculation of melting lines under extreme conditions. Using first-principles ab-initio DFT-MD simulations, I employ a heat-until-it-melts (HUM) approach to investigate the melting behavior of fcc and bcc gold above 1 TPa and perform static DFT calculations to model the equation-of-state (EoS) of Gold.

Sulfur

Melting Curve and Phase Diagram of Sulfur

Sulfur is relevant to inner-core crystallization and volcanism, yet its melting curve has been measured up to 65 GPa. Sulfur exhibits complex behavior between 0-120 GPa, undergoing amorphous, metallic and superconducting states, and is predicted to undergo phase transitions similar to other group VI elements: (1) base-centered orthorhombic around 90 GPa, (2) β-Po rhombohedral around 212 GPa, to (3) bcc around 550 GPa. Using DFT-MD, I construct and simulate various sulfur crystal phases to calculate the melting curve using the HUM approach.

Seismology

I work on observational earthquake science projects, interested in ground-motion and source physics projects. I am passionate about working on seismology projects with more direct applications toward helping people and communities in earthquake prone regions.

Ground-motion

Ground Motions in Cape Mendocino

In December of 2024 a M7.0 earthquake off-shore Cape Mendocino produced strong ground-motions and shaking that was felt from southern Oregon to south of the Bay Area. Despite being California's most seismically active region, it is unclear how the observed ground motions compare to those from typical California crustal earthquakes, as well as subduction zone events. For the past year, I have worked with the U.S. Geological Survey Earthquake Science Center to investigate over 486 earthquakes of M3.0+, with 23 M5.0+ events to improve understanding of regional site and path effects. Using NGA-West2 and NGA-Subduction ground motion models, we investigate trends in peak ground acceleration and velocity, and conduct robust analysis to analyze the source, site, and path contributions. We consider if MTJ earthquakes are more closely modeled with crustal or subduction zone GMMs, and whether this necessitates more physically precise models or alterations to current models for better hazard characterization in the MTJ region. Using 3D fault modeling, we characterize the MFZ into 6 regions of similar geology, in order to more rigorously investigate potential relationships between the earthquake source and model performance.

Personal Projects

Fractals

REM Sleep Driven Newtonian Fractals

REM is a unique brain state with interesting features in EEG waves. How can we simulate these features in a newtonian fractal? For a class project, we were asked to design a simulation of any topic of interest. Sleep patterns have always been interesting to me, and processing most wave data is similar across seismic waves, sound waves, and even brain waves! I extracted band powers from the EEG waves using a Morlet wavelet transform, which gave me alpha, beta, theta, and delta. I developed a mapping framework by which I correlated the different band-powers to visual changes like zoom, geometric morphing, warping, brightness, and color changes.
